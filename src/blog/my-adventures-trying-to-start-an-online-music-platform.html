This article is about my on-and-off attempts to build online music platforms: an earlier minor-label style product that never reached profitability, and Playlisted, the project I am shipping now.

You will get what was fulfilling about the first attempt, what Playlisted already does well, where it still needs polish, and why I am seriously considering marketing it to AI music creators even while that market is getting hammered by real musicians.

The first platform felt like a label

Years ago I built another music platform. It worked more like a small label than a pure streaming clone. Find artists. Promote them online. Give the music a place to live and a reason for someone to press play.

It was exciting, fulfilling, and fun. It was also a lot of work. Discovery, relationships, content, distribution, and the endless small fires that come with any product that depends on people showing up. We did not reach profitability. That part still matters. Fun is not a business model by itself.

I left that chapter with a clear memory: music products are emotional when they work, and brutal when the economics do not. I never fully lost the itch.

Playlisted is the current cut

The current project is Playlisted. Charts, curated playlists, genres, and a listening experience that is trying to feel like a place instead of a spreadsheet of tracks.

A few pieces I am proud of already:

  • Semi-realistic radio — not just shuffle with branding. A station-like flow that can keep someone in the product without forcing them to curate every next click.
  • Decent admin controls — enough to run the catalog and editorial surface without living in the database.
  • Immersive playback — integrated background canvas plus video controls, with optional subtitles, so listening can feel like an environment instead of a tiny progress bar.

It still needs more polish. That is not false modesty. The bones are there. The product has not earned the kind of finish that makes a stranger trust it in the first thirty seconds.

And right now, my only songs on it are AI songs. That is both a limitation and a clue about who this version of the product might actually be for.

The AI music market is swelling under fire

I am strongly considering marketing Playlisted to AI music creators. That market is getting bigger while also getting deeply criticized by real musicians. Fair. A lot of AI music is thin, samey, and easy to dismiss. I have written about that hostility before. It is not imaginary.

Criticism is slowing the cultural acceptance. It is not stopping the rising interest in ultra-personal music. People are going to keep making it. Tools got cheaper. Taste got optional at the point of generation. Identity got injectable into a prompt. Whether you like that or not, the production side of the funnel is filling up.

The harder question is demand. Will anyone listen to it? Much less buy it?

Supply is easy. Attention is not.

AI creators do not primarily need another generator. They need distribution, packaging, charts, radio-like continuity, and a place that treats their catalog as real enough to present with some dignity. That is the bet I keep turning over with Playlisted.

If AI music stays a private amusement, platforms for it die. If even a fraction of that output finds listeners who return, then charts, playlists, immersive players, and lightweight label-style promotion start to matter again. Not because the songs are automatically good. Because someone has to turn a flood of files into an experience people can enter.

I do not know the answer yet. I know the first platform taught me that passion and grind are not enough without a buyer. I know Playlisted already has radio, admin, and an immersive shell worth finishing. And I know the creator side of AI music is not waiting for cultural permission.

So the adventure continues in a narrower form: polish the product, point it at the people already making this music, and find out whether listening follows making. If it does not, that is useful. If it does, Playlisted suddenly has a lane.
