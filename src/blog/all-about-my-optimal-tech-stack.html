This article is my usual tech stack: not a religion, not a one-size answer, just the defaults I reach for when I want a system that stays coherent under change.

You will get the contract spine I trust, how I pick servers and frameworks, when I keep the UI on the same host, what I actually use on the frontend now, and the quality tools that keep the whole thing from drifting.

It depends, then it does not

Of course it depends on the project. Mobile-only, heavy SEO, GPU workloads, regulated data, tiny prototypes. Different constraints win.

Still, I have a set of goodies I am accustomed to. When those are in place, the system has a hard time getting too wild.

Prisma, OpenAPI, Zod

Those three are the spine. Prisma owns the data model. OpenAPI owns the HTTP contract. Zod owns runtime validation and the typed shapes that travel through the app.

Together they answer the same questions in one place: what exists, what can be sent, what is valid. Handwritten twins of those facts are where drift starts. Keep the spine honest and a lot of architecture theater becomes unnecessary.

Servers: Fastify and the boring companions

For actual servers I have found Fastify, or something in that family, to be the least friction. Fast enough. Clear plugin model. Easy to keep thin.

Around that I still reach for tools like Helmet and Passport when the job is security headers and auth plumbing. Not because they are trendy. Because I do not want to invent those layers from scratch on every product.

Next or not

Whether to use Next is still an open question for me. It wins when the product is mostly a web app that wants routing, rendering, and deployment conventions in one box. It loses when I want a plain API server, a static Vite app, or a generator-owned architecture that already decides those seams.

I do not treat Next as mandatory modernity. I treat it as one possible packaging of frontend and server concerns.

Generators versus opinionated frameworks

If we are using my generator YAML-based architecture, the opinions live in the pipeline: schema to contracts to routes to clients. That is the point.

If we are not, I would rather use an opinionated framework in the StrongLoop tradition than a pile of unguided packages. Somebody has to decide the shape. Either the generator does, or the framework does. “Flexible forever” usually means every feature invents a slightly different server.

Serve the UI or detach it

Next decision point: same-host serving versus a detached frontend. There are positives for both. Separate deploys, separate scaling, cleaner mobile and multi-platform clients. Detach can be the right call.

Unless you are developing for mobile or multi-platform, I think the benefits of same-host serving outweigh the conveniences of detaching the frontend. One origin. Simpler auth cookies. Fewer CORS stories. Fewer “which environment is out of date” mornings. Detach when you need to. Do not detach because a blog post said micro-frontends sound mature.

Frontend: is there still a debate?

I feel like Angular is an antique, even though that is where all of my professional experience lives. Respect to the years. That is not where I build now.

All of my independent experience in the post-AI world is with React and Vite. Fast feedback. Small surface. Agents and humans both move through it without learning a cathedral. My next choices after that are vanilla or React Native, depending on whether the UI is a simple site or a real native client.

Since we already have Zod, we are in TypeScript land anyway. That part is not a debate for me.

Keep it rock solid

For static analysis, ESLint of course. I also like Fallow a lot: dead code, duplication, complexity, the structural drift that lint rules miss. Combined with e2e tests, your systems get pretty rock solid.

Lint catches local mistakes. Fallow catches architectural mess. E2E catches the product still working when the pieces are wired together. That trio is cheap insurance compared to rediscovering quality during an incident.

The default picture

Prisma, OpenAPI, Zod. Fastify-shaped servers with Helmet and Passport when needed. Generator pipeline when I have it, StrongLoop-style opinions when I do not. Same-host UI unless mobile or multi-platform forces a split. React and Vite for the web, vanilla or React Native when that fits better. TypeScript throughout. ESLint, Fallow, and e2e on the way out the door.

That is not the only stack that works. It is the stack that keeps me from rebuilding my judgment from scratch on every project.
