This is a biased opinion piece on why the public seems to hate AI music and AI media in general. I use generative tools, including Suno, and I have shipped AI video, so read it with that filter.

You will get the stack of reasons I think matter most: cheap/low-quality output, fear of what people do not understand, tech fatigue, data-center arguments, and what it would take to move public opinion.

Cheap reads as low quality

Generative AI is cheap. A lot of what it produces looks and sounds like a low-quality product. The public hates that. Not in an abstract policy way. In a gut way. If it feels mass-produced, thin, and effortless, people reject it before they debate the ethics.

People are right that a lot of it is still low-effort and low-quality. I am not arguing otherwise. Even ardent opponents of AI media may mostly be people who have not been moved yet by anything good enough to force a second look.

Fear of what you do not understand

There is also the classic pattern: fearing what you do not understand. Public perception of AI hovers somewhere between science fiction and Revelation. Machines writing songs. Machines making faces. Machines coming for work, taste, and meaning. That fog makes every mediocre output feel like a threat instead of a draft.

What I hear is mostly from peers over forty. I assume a lot of people under twenty are enjoying this shift the way we enjoyed the internet and mobile: local LLMs, free credits, dreaming about shaping the world with tools that suddenly feel available.

Technology fatigue

I also think the public is technology fatigued. We used to get genuinely excited when Intel released a new chipset. Dial-up to broadband mattered. HD to 4K fascinated people. Now the releases keep coming and the emotional return feels flatter.

Does that just seem true because I was younger then? Maybe. I may have been too young to catch the internet hate. I definitely felt the hostility during the adoption of digital film: told you were not a real photographer if you leaned on composition tools, not a real musician for using samples. Now the same argument is aimed at AI.

Maybe the difference is simple. So few people have impressed the public yet with AI-native work that nobody has to take it seriously. Until that happens, dismissal is easy.

Data centers were already hot

On data center expansion, my view is blunt. That growth is happening with or without AI. We are not going back to cable. If you think this is bad, wait until Netflix and Amazon decide to broadcast 8K as the default expectation.

I rack a lot of that expansion up to Moore's law and rising SLAs. Out here I keep trying to remind people that data centers were very hot places long before generative AI. We are adding more chips because that is how you keep up with demand that was already climbing.

Waiting for the breakthrough

So yes: I am biased. A lot of AI music and AI video still deserves the skepticism it gets. Cheap tools make it easy to publish unfinished taste.

What I am watching for is the first breakthrough that is good enough to turn public opinion. Not a policy memo. A song, a film, a piece of media that makes the dismissal feel outdated overnight. Until then, the hate makes sense from the outside. From the inside, I am still building with the tools and waiting for that bar to move.
