This article is about adding a vector database to my toolkit via Pinecone: when data will not fit a context window, retrieval becomes necessary, and my use case was chatting over hundreds of pages of messy scrum spreadsheet data.

You will get the RAG shape we used, how fast ingest felt, why retrieval held up on uneven text, and how I place vector stores beside relational, document, and graph databases.

The use case

My project was importing a team's daily scrum messages into Pinecone and attaching that index to a chat interface. The starting point was hundreds of pages of mismatched spreadsheet data. Different formats, uneven columns, notes that only made sense to the person who wrote them that morning.

Product and program management needed a way to ask questions across all of it. Not by hunting through tabs. By chatting with an assistant that could find the relevant status updates, blockers, and history on demand.

That is a classic RAG shape: ingest the messy corpus, embed it, retrieve the nearest context, then answer. Pinecone was the retrieval layer.

What surprised me

Ingestion is fast now. What used to feel like a data migration takes less than a minute for our load. The vector database slices through messy input and lets the model decide which fragments belong together for a given question.

For our use case, that retrieval was nearly flawless. Not because the spreadsheets were clean. Because semantic search does not need the rows to agree with each other first. It needs embeddings that preserve meaning well enough to surface the right neighborhood of text.

That is the real pitch. You stop boiling the ocean of spreadsheet cleanup before anyone can ask a useful question. You ingest, index, and let relevance do the first pass.

Where it sits for me

I have set up a couple of RAG systems with DH. This was probably the most complicated one: noisy operational text, mixed authorship, daily cadence, and a chat UI that had to feel trustworthy to people who already lived in those spreadsheets.

Relational databases still own structure. Document stores still own flexible records. Graphs still own relationships. Vector databases own "find me the meaning near this question" at a scale the prompt window cannot hold.

Once you have a corpus that large and that uneven, chatting with the archive stops being a demo and starts being the interface.
