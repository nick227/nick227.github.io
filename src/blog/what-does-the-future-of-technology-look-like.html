This article is my read on where technology is headed: the death of the open web, the platform consolidation that was already underway, and what AI is doing to the leftover pieces.

You will get the platform map as I see it, the intellectual-property argument that already settled, the strange creator economy paradox, what this feels like for software engineers, and why I still think AI is mostly a very cool database wearing a costume.

The death of websites

Even before AI, the consolidation of users onto three to five platforms was already an extinction-level event for websites. Attention left the open web. Discovery left the open web. Commerce followed. Independent sites became optional destinations that fewer people needed to visit.

AI is a meteor shower of nuclear warheads on top of that. Google is not even really in the website business anymore. The old deal — publish a page, get indexed, earn a visit — is rotting in public.

There is still the app market. People are still creating digital products to put onto the major platforms. Hopefully there are still jobs with those platforms. In that picture Google becomes the new AT&T, General Electric, and network TV. Netflix and Apple become the new cable.

Is there a place for Facebook?

Yes. For advertisers, Facebook and Instagram are even bigger than before now that Google has self-destructed its AdSense business. The feed still owns attention. The ads still own the budget that used to scatter across a million small sites.

Even though I feel the metaverse was a flop, Meta glasses are being sold at every Best Buy. The technology is amazing. If and when that space emerges for real, they will be a top-three player in it. Flops do not always mean the company exits the next round.

IP was already settled

The question of intellectual property rights for OpenAI and AI service companies was pretty firmly settled by Google already. If you put something open on the internet, that thing is open to spiders.

I think the public imagination is running a little wild and underestimating how many digital collections OpenAI pays premium prices for. Free content is less than the Google Images trash of the web. They are not getting into authenticated content they cannot get past a simple robots: ignore.

The real problem is the loss of organic incentive for human content, while ironically the content-creator industry explodes. Everyone wants to get Instagram famous. Publishing for the open web and publishing for the feed are not the same job anymore.

Software engineers as ronin

The outlook for software engineers feels bleak. We feel like ronin out here: samurai with no master. Not because we are getting replaced by AI. Because this drying up of opportunity is hitting the big companies, and they are the first to respond to it.

When platforms consolidate and headcount follows revenue concentration, the independent craftsperson feels the weather change first in hiring freezes and thin pipelines, not in a chatbot writing their functions.

Is the future AI-enhanced?

Or does AI slowly shuffle off the stage, rebuffed back to the status of machine learning? Most likely so. That is where it belongs. That is what it was the whole time. I have been trying to tell people: AI is just a really cool database.

Natural language processing combined with reinforcement learning is amazing. Fundamentally, AI is not going to come up with something a human did not put in there in the first place. AI has no concept of continuity even from one nanosecond to the next. Artificial intelligence is to intelligence what an artificial dog is to a real dog.

Google's Gemini pivot

Google putting Gemini at the top of google.com is an earth-shattering decision that nobody is really talking about, because we get it. They were not about to fade into irrelevance when they have the best AI IP in the world.

What are they going to do for their next act? Probably nothing. Probably wait and see for the next ten years. Corporations are in a holding pattern right now. We just veered closer to a post-work economy than ever before. That is every person's core challenge: how to turn work into a hobby, how to reduce your daily commute.

What I still want to see

I hope we see major engineering breakthroughs in NPU technology. New players. A reshaped data-center market. We need some good news to offset rising hardware overhead.

I am still curious whether the MCP revolution is going to happen or not. The full broad AI automation wave is starting to remind me of the Internet of Everything: huge promise, uneven delivery, a lot of slide decks.

That aside, AI already obviously disrupted the market. I am fortunate to have had a front-row seat during the internet and AI explosions. Let's hope the next one is even bigger. The most obvious candidate is a better metaverse that externalizes the web into the 3D world.

Let me know when I can virtually spray-paint my name onto the roof of my house.
