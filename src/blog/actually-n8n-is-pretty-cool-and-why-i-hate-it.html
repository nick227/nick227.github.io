This article is about n8n as a solo automation tool: powerful event orchestration that centralizes auth and turns repeated CLI glue into a visual graph, plus the real reason I stepped away after losing workflows.

You will get where it shines for one-person projects, a concrete multi-agent content flow, the awkward parts, and why backup discipline matters more than the node UX complaints.

Developers shy away from n8n because it feels clunky to bring online. For team infrastructure, that friction matters. For individual projects where I am the only user, it is amazingly helpful. Centralizing authentication for providers and servers alone is worth the effort. The node-based visual system is what most of my automation projects would become eventually anyway. I usually start in the CLI, then rewrite the same glue an hour later. Putting those blinders on and focusing on the prompts frees time for the part that actually matters.

You still get to code

This is not a no-code sermon. n8n supports inline JavaScript and can call any kind of script. When a task gets too complex, break out a helper and call it from a node. Keep the orchestration visual. Keep the hard logic in real code. That split works.

I used n8n to stand up half a dozen blogs, on subjects from AI content to movie and music reviews. I scraped financial data and YouTube channels and published financial and real-estate content. Webhooks, chat, agents, timers, credentials. The free version already has enough surface area that I strongly advise developers to give it an honest try.

A basic flow

One of my common patterns was simple. Connect to Google Sheets. Keep a list of blog ideas with a status column. On a timer, n8n checks the sheet for new rows and processes them one at a time. A single row could hit dozens of LLM calls before it was done.

For song analysis, I passed in the name of a song and let specialized agents take sections. One wrote about the genre. Another covered lyrics, beats, and timing. Another generated how to play that song on guitar. We had sections that dove into the artist. An agent generated the thumbnail. I generated voice, and at times talking video, to read an excerpt or a welcome message. LLM nodes and code nodes, chained together, still leaving room to validate the work before it went live.

That is the appeal. You stop rebuilding the same pipeline and start composing the content system.

Where it gets awkward

Inner loops are tricky and cumbersome. Nesting iteration inside iteration in a visual graph gets messy fast. That is a real limitation, and it slowed me down more than once.

That is not what turned me off n8n.

What actually did it was losing my workflows. It is a long story. The short version is enough: after losing hours of work, I have not been able to go back into n8n.

So this is both recommendation and warning. Use it. Centralize your auth. Build the graph you were going to invent in code anyway. Export and back up your workflows like they are source code, because for solo automation they are. The platform is cool. Losing the work is what made me hate it.
