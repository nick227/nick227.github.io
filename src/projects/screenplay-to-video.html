The pipeline runs five stages: screenplay parsing, where an LLM extracts a scene graph; shot planning, where an agent converts action lines into generation prompts; a Redis-managed GPU queue running LTX locally; continuity resolution across adjacent shots; and assembly, where ffmpeg stitches clips with transitions and audio. Video generation runs on local GPU hardware, so the only ongoing cost is the LLM calls for parsing and planning.

Most AI video tools at the time handled single clips well but struggled with multi-scene narratives. I built the coordination layer — the agent that breaks a screenplay into a consistent shot sequence, the queue that manages parallel GPU jobs, and the assembly step that turns the clips into a finished video.
